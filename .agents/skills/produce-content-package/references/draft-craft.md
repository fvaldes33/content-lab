# Draft Craft Rules

Distilled from tested practice, not theory — each rule earned its place against real retention and engagement data during this system's first weeks. When a rule here conflicts with generic social-media advice, this file wins.

## The hook (seconds 0–2 decide everything)

- Retention data from this system's own posts: roughly half of viewers leave in the first second. The hook is not an intro — it is the product.
- **Person + stake + specific true number** beats process description, every time. "Nobody knows who I am. A week ago I started from zero…" hooks; "Here's how my pipeline works" does not.
- Cold open: the first word lands on the first frame. No greeting, no breath, no context-setting.
- Never open with a taxonomy, a definition, or the word "so."

## The body

- **Specific > vague.** "Fifteen sources behind sixty seconds of video," never "well-researched." If you don't have the specific number, earn it or cut the claim.
- **Short. Breathe. Land.** One idea per sentence. Vary lengths hard. A queue of drafts that all share one skeleton reads as manufactured even when every fact is true — check the set, not just each line.
- One concrete example or observed moment minimum. Abstract advice is skippable; a moment is not.

## The close (the value beat belongs to the viewer)

- End on what the viewer gets, not on the creator's feelings, humility, or journey. Honesty beats about your own small numbers or killed posts are true and brand-building — put them in the caption, where engaged readers find them, not in scarce final seconds.
- Self-demonstration is the strongest close available: if the artifact proves the claim by existing, say so.
- A comment-gate CTA is permitted only when the deliverable already exists.

## Length

- Default 30–60 seconds. Every second past 60 must fight for its life; completion rate feeds distribution on every platform.
