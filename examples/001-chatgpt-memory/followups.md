# Follow-ups and Learning Plan

Reframed 2026-08-16 with the failure-first, cross-product angle.

## Content hypothesis

Opening on a failure the viewer has personally experienced, explaining the mechanism in one line, and paying off with product-verified fixes will earn more saves and shares than a taxonomy explainer — and covering ChatGPT and Claude together will widen reach without weakening Franco's practitioner credibility.

## Audience behavior to watch

- **Primary expected behavior:** Saves (the fixes are reference material) and shares to people who complain about AI forgetting things; non-followers watching through to the delete-chat kicker.
- **Useful questions or objections:** "How do I see what it knows about me?", "Does this work on the free plan?", "What about Gemini?", "Is Incognito actually private?", "Why did it forget something in the same short chat?", or corrections from viewers whose interface differs mid-rollout.
- **Response that supports the hypothesis:** Viewers reporting they audited their memory page; specific fix-related questions using the two-memory vocabulary; requests for the deeper walkthrough or the Gemini comparison.
- **Response that weakens it:** Drop-off before the first fix; comments engaging only with the creepy hook and not the fixes; confusion between the three failures suggesting the grouping didn't land.
- **Signs the hook attracted the wrong audience:** The conversation centers on surveillance conspiracies or AI sentience rather than product behavior and user control.

## Natural follow-ups

1. **The full memory audit walkthrough** — screen-recorded pass through both products' memory pages, deletion steps, and what actually gets removed. Earned if viewers ask "how do I check?"
2. **Where did ChatGPT get that? The four-source provenance model** — the original P01 angle, revived as a vocabulary post. Earned if comments show provenance confusion ("but HOW did it know that?").
3. **Memory vs Custom Instructions vs Projects** — when to use which, in both products. Earned if the failure-3 fix draws questions.
4. **What Temporary/Incognito chats do and don't promise** — retention nuance in both products. Requires a fresh official-source check; earned if privacy questions recur.
5. **The same three failures in Gemini** — earned if "what about Gemini?" recurs; requires new research, not an assumption of symmetry.

## Possible business signal

- **What might be economically meaningful:** Repeated requests from professionals or teams for a memory/privacy audit of their AI setup, a trustworthy configuration guide, or training on how organizational AI memory and data retention behave (the Claude Team/Enterprise retention caveat is a natural wedge into org-level questions).
- **What would justify an opportunity-inbox entry:** Three independent people requesting substantially the same deeper resource or help; a qualified buyer asking for team training or an audit with independent evidence of the organizational problem; or repeated reports of costly mistakes caused by misunderstood memory behavior.
- **What is merely engagement:** "AI is scary," generic likes, one person asking for more, high views, or debate about consciousness.

Do not convert anticipated interest into a business claim. Update this file with observed behavior after publication.
